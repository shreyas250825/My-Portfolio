Federated Learning · 6G Wireless March 2026 – November 2026

Federated Unlearning for Neural Equalizers in 6G Wireless Communication Systems

Indian Institute of Technology (BHU), Varanasi  ·  Research Intern

Investigating client-level federated unlearning in neural receivers for next-generation wireless communication systems. The work examines how a federated learning system can respond to deletion requests while preserving the utility of the retained model, evaluated against a retained-only reference.

Methodology
  • Neural equalization in wireless receivers
  • Federated learning and client-level deletion
  • Forgetting effectiveness evaluation
  • Canary-based evaluation framework
  • Equivalence testing against retained-only baseline
  • Comparison of federated unlearning approaches
Machine Unlearning Neural Equalization 6G TOST Federated Systems
Federated Learning · Aggregation June 2026 – November 2026

AdaptFlux: A Server-Side Aggregation Method for Heterogeneous Federated Learning

Formulation, Corrections, and Controlled Evaluation

An investigation into server-side aggregation approaches for federated learning under label heterogeneity and non-IID data distributions. This work includes algebraic examination of proposed formulations, identification of mathematical limitations, and development of corrected aggregation components.

Contributions
  • Algebraic examination of representation-aware weighting
  • Analysis of gradient-divergence correction mechanisms
  • Identification of mathematical limitations in original formulation
  • Development of corrected aggregation components
  • Controlled evaluation across non-IID data conditions
  • Statistical analysis and reproducible experimentation
This work does not establish that the combined approach consistently outperforms simpler alternatives. The contribution lies in analytical corrections, rigorous evaluation, and critical assessment.
Heterogeneous FL Non-IID Data Aggregation Statistical Analysis
Constraint Optimization · Scheduling March 2025 – May 2026

A Workload-Aware Constraint-Based Framework for Automated Academic Timetable Generation

Research into constraint-based academic timetable generation with emphasis on feasible scheduling, faculty allocation, and workload fairness. Evaluated using an institutional dataset and structurally derived synthetic datasets.

Framework Design
  • Eligible faculty identification and workload-aware assignment
  • Enforcement of institutional hard constraints
  • Comparison against a conventional First-Fit baseline
  • Evaluation of scheduling completeness and feasibility
  • Ablation analysis and scalability evaluation
Constraint Satisfaction Scheduling Optimization Ablation Study
NLP · Statistical Analysis June 2026 – November 2026

Sampling Noise and the Consistency of Online Sentiment

An Analysis of the Sentiment Consistency Index, a Sample-Size-Aware Correction, and Evidence from Real Text

Research investigating the statistical reliability of sentiment-consistency measurements in online textual streams. Examines mathematical limitations of the original Sentiment Consistency Index and proposes sample-size-aware statistical corrections.

Methodology
  • Mathematical analysis of the original Sentiment Consistency Index
  • Bounded consistency formulations
  • Sample-size-aware statistical corrections
  • Logistic-regression-based sentiment classification
  • Timestamp-permutation null models
  • Semi-synthetic resampling for robustness evaluation
