Federated Learning · Non-IID Data · Distributed Optimization

AdaptFlux: A Server-Side Aggregation Method for Heterogeneous Federated Learning – Formulation, Corrections, and Controlled Evaluation

First AuthorUnder Review

Analysed representation-aware weighting, gradient-divergence correction, and elastic knowledge retention; identified participation bias, exponential filtering behaviour, and unbounded parameter-scale dependence. Formulated AdaptFlux-v1 using exposure-aware weighting and a bounded pull towards a moving-average anchor.

Compared against FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, q-FedAvg, and matched controls across 3,019 runs on Digits, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10.

Methodology and selected findings
  • Worst-client accuracy improvements over FedAvg were 3.2, 7.3, and 7.4 percentage points at Dirichlet alpha = 0.1, but none survived Holm correction.
  • Under cohort shift, forgetting was 12.4 percentage points for AdaptFlux-v1, compared with 20.0 for FedAvg and 11.9 for uniform weighting.
  • These findings are limited to the controlled experiments and do not establish consistent superiority over all existing federated-learning algorithms.

Constraint-Based Scheduling · Greedy Algorithms · Faculty Workload Optimization

A Workload-Aware Constraint-Based Framework for Automated Academic Timetable Generation

First AuthorUnder Review

Developed a constraint-based scheduling method that enforces institutional hard constraints, prioritizes feasible faculty assignments according to existing workloads, and compares the method with a fixed-order First-Fit baseline.

Methodology and selected findings
  • Evaluated using real institutional data, structurally derived synthetic datasets, and ablation studies.
  • Generated complete schedules with zero hard-constraint violations on the real institutional dataset.
  • Reduced faculty workload standard deviation from 38.76 to 7.09.
  • Ablation analysis showed the importance of removing fixed candidate-order bias and introducing workload-aware prioritization.

Statistical NLP · Sentiment Analysis · Sampling Variance · Empirical Evaluation

Sampling Noise and the Consistency of Online Sentiment: An Analysis of the Sentiment Consistency Index, a Sample-Size-Aware Correction, and Evidence from Real Text

First AuthorUnder Review

Analysed mathematical limitations of the original Sentiment Consistency Index, including undefined behaviour, unbounded values, and confounding between sentiment level and dispersion. Evaluated logistic regression on 2,000 movie reviews and 9,400 emoticon-labelled tweets, and analysed 5,526 real tweets across 62 one-minute bins using timestamp-permutation null models and semi-synthetic resampling.

Methodology and selected findings
  • Movie-review macro-F1: 0.879; tweet-classification macro-F1: 0.753.
  • Bounded consistency index AUC: 0.931; original prototype AUC: 0.512.
  • Sample-size-aware correction AUC: 0.942 for equal-sized bins.
  • Results included near-duplicate leakage correction and remain subject to limitations under unequal bin sizes and within-bin duplication.

Explainable AI · Deep Learning · Wireless Signal Processing

Explainable Deep Learning for Physical-Layer Classification of Wi-Fi, LTE and 5G-NR Signals Using SHAP

Second AuthorIn Preparation

Contributed to methodology, software, formal analysis, visualization, and manuscript drafting. Investigated a four-hidden-layer deep neural network using 24 magnitude-, phase-, and spectrum-derived features from 292,955 IQ segments. Analysed feature importance using SHAP and examined preprocessing leakage, evaluation-set reuse, feature dependence, and recording independence.

Recorded results and limitations
  • Recorded accuracy: 91.68%; macro-F1: 91.57%; 58,591 evaluation segments.
  • These are recorded experimental results, not independently validated generalization results.
  • A source-disjoint validation protocol was outlined; unseen-session generalization is not claimed.

Federated Unlearning · Neural Equalizers · 6G Wireless Communication

Federated Unlearning for Neural Equalizers in 6G Wireless Communication Systems

First AuthorUnder Review

Investigated client-level federated unlearning for neural equalizers in a heterogeneous wireless communication environment involving nine clients and symbol-wise 256-QAM modulation.

Methodology and selected findings
  • Evaluated FedEraser, gradient ascent, and retained-data fine-tuning.
  • Used a controlled forgetting canary and retained-only reference model with Gold-relative equivalence testing and five random seeds.
  • In the specific five-seed canary experiments, FedEraser reduced canary attack success from 91.39% to 0.00%, compared with 0.01% for Gold, 24.44% for gradient ascent, and 79.38% for retained-data fine-tuning.
  • These findings apply to the documented experiment and do not imply universally guaranteed machine unlearning.

Biomedical AI · Computational Cardiology · ECG Signal Processing · Embedded AI

Patient-Specific Predictive Cardiac Digital Twin for Early Ventricular Arrhythmia Forecasting

Conference SubmissionUnder Review

Investigates patient-specific ventricular arrhythmia forecasting using ECG beat classification and retrospective forecasting windows, together with PYNQ-Z2 ARM inference and a team-developed visualization dashboard.

Recorded results and scope
  • Normal/Ventricular classification: 99.27% accuracy, 95.52% balanced accuracy, and 0.9950 ROC-AUC across 43,593 test beats.
  • 30-minute absolute-feature Transformer average precision: 0.3208.
  • NumPy export verification achieved 100% class agreement for four-class and N/V models.
  • This is a research prototype and retrospective evaluation, not a clinically validated device or clinical deployment.

Patient-Specific Predictive Cardiac Digital Twin for Early Ventricular Arrhythmia Forecasting

Developed a 259-feature XGBoost ECG classifier using 41 MIT-BIH recordings. Investigated patient-specific modelling using 656 windows and 12 absolute and baseline-deviation features, and retrospective ventricular-fibrillation forecasting at 5–30-minute horizons using 464 windows from 20 SDDB records.

Investigated PYNQ-Z2 ARM inference, developed a React-based visualization dashboard as part of the team implementation, and verified exported NumPy model predictions.

Recorded results and scope

  • NumPy export verification achieved 100% class agreement for four-class and N/V models.

These results distinguish ECG beat classification from retrospective arrhythmia forecasting. This work is a research prototype and retrospective evaluation, not a clinically validated medical device or a documented clinical deployment.
